Shop notes

Extension cords: match the gauge to the tool and keep it temporary. Benchtop planers, miter saws and shop vacs pull serious current, and an undersized cord runs hot and starves the motor. The Electrical Safety Foundation International (ESFI) gauge chart: for 25–50 ft, 16-gauge is fine up to 13 A, 14-gauge for 14–15 A and 12–10-gauge for 16–20 A; at 100 ft, step up to 14-gauge for 11–13 A and 12-gauge for 14–15 A. Use one tool per cord, never daisy-chain power strips, don’t run cords under rugs or through doorways, retire any cord that feels hot or has nicked insulation, and never cut off the ground pin. If you live on extension cords, add outlets. Source: ESFI — Extension Cord Safety Tips.

Build Tips

1. Read the grain before the planer does. Fine Woodworking’s planer guide (Jerry Forshee) starts with the simplest fix for tearout: look at the board’s edge and feed it so the grain runs “downhill” into the cutterhead. Keep each pass to 1/16″ or less (lighter still on wide boards in a benchtop planer), and for wild or reversing grain, feed the board in at a slight skew to get a shearing cut. Once both faces are parallel, flip the board end for end between passes so you take wood evenly off both faces and it stays flat. Wax the bed and tables, and keep dust collection running, because loose chips can dimple the surface. A helical head like the JET above reduces tearout but doesn’t replace these habits.

Fine Woodworking — Get Better Cuts With Your Planer (Jerry Forshee)

2. (Staining/finish coloring) Keep end grain from staining dark. End grain soaks up stain and turns much darker than the face, so a rail-and-stile door can look like two different woods. Popular Woodworking’s fix is to sand the end grain finer than the faces: in its test, end grain taken to 320 grit (faces at 180) stained almost the same color as the face grain. The Wood Whisperer (Marc Spagnuolo) gives the same 180/320 rule, plus an alternative for thirsty woods: pre-seal the end grain with a light coat of 2-lb-cut shellac or glue size (PVA thinned about 90% with water), let it dry, sand lightly, then stain. Always test on an offcut from the same board first.
