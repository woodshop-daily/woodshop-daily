Shop notes

Charge cordless batteries safely: With a half-price M18 pack and several bare tools on today’s list, a reminder from the NFPA: lithium-ion batteries are most likely to overheat or catch fire when they’re damaged or improperly charged or stored. Use only the charger made for your battery platform, stop charging once a pack is full, and stop using any pack that smells odd, gets unusually hot, pops, swells or changes color. Don’t throw dead packs in the trash or recycling — take them to a battery recycling or household-hazardous-waste drop-off. Source: NFPA — Lithium-Ion Battery Safety.

Build Tips

1. Get pocket-hole joints to close flush. Set the jig and drill-bit stop collar to the thickness of the board that gets the pockets, then pick screw length from the jig’s chart. Use coarse-thread pocket screws in pine, poplar, plywood and MDF, and fine-thread screws in hardwoods. Clamp across the joint and down to a flat surface so the faces can’t creep out of line as the screw snugs up, and drive with a drill on a low clutch setting, not an impact driver, which can strip the hole before you feel it. Add glue unless you’ll need to take the joint apart later.

WOOD Magazine — Pocket-Hole Pointers

2. (Staining/finish coloring) Even out color with a dye toner. When stain leaves the wood too light or the boards don’t match, tone instead of re-staining. After the sealer coat, spray finish with a little dye added. Dye darkens without muddying the grain the way pigment can. Thin the finish and colorant a lot (as much as six times) so color builds slowly. Add a touch of red or orange to warm the color, or green or black to cool it. Mist only the light areas first, such as sapwood, then a light pass over the whole piece, and protect it with a clear topcoat.

Popular Woodworking (Bob Flexner) — 7 Ways to Get a Darker Color
