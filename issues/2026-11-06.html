Shop notes

Table saw kickback: keep the riving knife on and stand out of the line of fire. OSHA’s woodworking eTool flags kickback as a primary table-saw hazard — the blade can throw a workpiece back at the operator when the kerf closes or the stock twists into the blade. Use the riving knife (or splitter) that came with the saw, keep anti-kickback pawls engaged for rip cuts when the design allows, and never reach over a spinning blade to free a pinched board. Feed with a push stick or push block past the blade, set the blade only slightly above the stock, and stand to the side of the cut line. Source: OSHA eTool — Table Saws; OSHA 2230 — Hand and Power Tools.

Build Tips

1. Keep a belt sander flat and moving. A belt sander removes stock fast, which is why it also digs divots when you pause or tip the platen. WOOD Magazine’s belt-sander guidance is simple: keep both hands on the tool, start the belt off the work, set it down flat, and keep it moving with the grain. Overlap passes by about half a belt width, let the weight of the tool do the cutting instead of leaning in, and ease off before you lift so you don’t leave a nose dig. For edges and scribes, clamp the workpiece and use a light front-handle grip so the platen stays level. Finish with a random-orbit sander once the surface is flat.

WOOD Magazine — Belt-Sanding Basics · Fine Woodworking — Belt Sanders

2. (Staining/finish coloring) Washcoat blotch-prone woods before liquid stain. Pine, cherry, maple and birch drink stain unevenly. Popular Woodworking (Bob Flexner) and WOOD Magazine both recommend a thin washcoat — wood conditioner or a heavily thinned finish — so the stain can’t dump into earlywood pores. Brush the washcoat on generously, wipe the excess, and let it dry fully (often overnight for oil/varnish conditioners) before staining. Gel stain is the other easy path: wipe it on, wait a few minutes, then wipe with the grain, pressing harder on dark spots. For the most control, seal with a 1-lb-cut shellac washcoat, scuff lightly, then apply dye or pigment stain.

Popular Woodworking — Sealers & Washcoats · WOOD Magazine — Avoid (or Fix) Blotchy Stain
