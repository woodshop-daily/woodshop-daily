Shop notes

Kickback on the table saw: With a new miter saw, drill press and dust collector on sale, it’s a good week to tune up the saw you already own. OSHA’s woodworking eTool notes kickback is most likely when ripping: keep the riving knife or spreader and anti-kickback pawls installed, use a sharp blade and a push stick, feed against the blade rotation, and stand to the side of the blade line rather than directly behind it. Source: OSHA — Woodworking eTool: Kickbacks.

Build Tips

1. Let the top move under its breadboard ends. A breadboard end keeps a solid top flat, but the top still swells and shrinks across its width. Cut a long tenon (or tongue) across the end of the panel and glue only the middle couple of inches. Drill the peg holes through the breadboard, then elongate the outer holes in the tenon side to side so those pegs can slide as the panel moves; only the center peg is fixed. Leave the outer pegs unglued in the tenon, and make the breadboard a hair long so it stays flush in the dry season.

Popular Woodworking — How to Make Breadboard Ends

2. (Staining/finish coloring) Glaze between coats for depth. Glazing adds color over a sealed surface instead of into bare wood. Seal with one or two coats of a film finish (shellac, lacquer or varnish, not oil or wax), brush on a glaze or gel stain, then wipe it off the raised areas while it’s still wet so color stays in the recesses of moldings, carvings and raised panels. Oil-based glaze gives you more working time and wipes off with mineral spirits if you don’t like the look. Let it dry, then apply at least one more coat of finish so the glaze can’t rub off.
