Shop notes

Spraying finishes at home: If that $24.99 HVLP gun is your first sprayer, treat the overspray like the hazard it is — OSHA notes spray finishing exposes you to toxic materials and flammable or explosive mists and vapors, and that spray booths exist both to protect the sprayer’s health and to cut fire and explosion risk. In a home shop that means exhausting air to the outdoors, keeping ignition sources (pilot lights, space heaters) away, and wearing an organic-vapor respirator. Source: OSHA — Spray Operations: Controlling Hazards.

Build Tips

1. Drawbore your mortise-and-tenons. Bore the peg hole through both mortise walls (about 3/8″ from the mortise opening on furniture), then mark the tenon’s hole offset toward the shoulder — 1/16″ for small work, about 3/32″ for furniture. A tapered, dry, straight-grained oak peg pulls the shoulder tight mechanically, even if the cheeks aren’t a piston fit.

Popular Woodworking — Drawboring Resurrected (Christopher Schwarz)

2. (Staining/finish coloring) Make a step sample board before you stain. Use a cutoff of each wood in the project (at least 7″ square per step), sand it exactly like the workpiece — a stain on pine at P150 looks very different at P400 — then apply stain and build clear coats to the final sheen in labeled sections so you see the true “wet” color.

Fine Woodworking — Sample Boards: No Surprises (Teri Masaschi)
