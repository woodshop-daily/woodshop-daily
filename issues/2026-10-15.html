Shop notes

Hand and power tool safety: A $90 circular saw and a $29.99 belt sander are only as safe as how you use them. OSHA notes that hand and power tools are present in nearly every workplace and can cause severe injuries when used or maintained improperly — so inspect cords, batteries, blades and guards before every session, keep guards in place, and disconnect power before changing bits or blades. Build Tips

1. Wedged through-mortise and tenon (tusk tenon). For knockdown frames and bed rails, bore and pare a through mortise, cut a tenon that protrudes past the post, then cut a tapered wedge-mortise in the tenon whose baseline sits slightly toward the shoulder (not on the post face) so the wedge bears on the post and locks the joint — disassemble by driving the wedge back out.

Popular Woodworking — The Wedged Mortise & Tenon (Peter Follansbee)

2. (Staining/finish coloring) Fill open pores before building a glossy stained finish. On oak, ash and mahogany, paste wood filler locks color into the pores for a glass-smooth topcoat. Seal or stain first if you want contrast, pack oil-based filler into the pores with a brush or burlap, wipe the haze across the grain, let dry overnight, then sand 320 and topcoat — seal oil filler with dewaxed shellac before a water-based finish.

Fine Woodworking — Filling Pores (Jeff Jewitt)
